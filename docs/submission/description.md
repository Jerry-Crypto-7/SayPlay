**Current local submission description — 17 September 2026.** Focus category: procedural worlds. Registration and public submission are not verified; this text does not assert acceptance of the remaining product gates.

Describe a place, act in its world, rewind, preview one rule change, confirm it, and compare two synchronized futures. WORLDSTREAM turns the request into a semantic plan and hybrid 3D scene: approved GLB/PBR objects, parametric architecture and terrain, and SDF geometry for unusual forms. A deterministic runtime executes declarative rules; the model never runs generated code.

The main page starts live generation for your own prompt. A provisional layout is explicitly noninteractive until rules are accepted. **Prepared demo** is a separate labeled choice, including exact matches. An unavailable live service does not silently return a prepared world.

A4 comparison is on the main page, with shared time and camera. It supports bounded compatible changes to an existing rule condition, action or timer; unsupported structural changes are refused. The alternative is accepted as `comparison-only`, with no new winning certificate, and can be compared even when it does not win.

The [90-second local video](../../videos/worldstream-a4/worldstream-a4-live.mp4) records a real live reading-room generation and model-proposed terminal intervention. In this single observation the accepted world took **258.445 s**, followed by **8.555 s** for the A4 path. Both futures were `won`; the terminal's existing signal was on in one and off in the other. The first 260 seconds of source footage are openly sped up 5×. Browser footage was captured at 800 × 450; the closing screenshot is 1920 × 1080.

This is an automated production-browser demonstration, not an independent human study, a public-service claim, a percentile measurement or evidence of general prompt fidelity. The latency target was not met by this observation. Visual quality, broader generation reliability, human evaluation, provider and hosting evidence remain separate open requirements. Structural checks and replay cannot certify the meaning or appearance of a generated world.

# WORLDSTREAM

> Draft status, 17 September 2026: the paragraphs below describe the earlier submission baseline and are not ready for publication. Current local work uses a hybrid GLB/PBR, parametric and SDF compiler and main-page A4 comparison with synchronized futures. Prepared worlds remain labeled even for exact matches. Public runtime, new live acceptance results, human review and final video are pending. Registration remains unverified. Replace the historical claims below only after the current variant is accepted; do not submit this draft as a completed product description.

**Focus kategorie:** procedural worlds

Registration is not verified.

A sentence in plain English becomes a 3D world you can look at and that runs. The model writes two declarative programs — the composition of the space, and the shape of its hero objects as signed distance fields that the browser meshes itself. A deterministic runtime executes those rules; the model never runs generated code. A second sentence changes one object of the world that is already standing, instead of building a new one.

Judges type their own sentences, so the product is built to answer a sentence it has never seen. A sentence the store does not recognise is answered by the nearest authored world, and the product labels that substitution on screen instead of passing it off as fresh generation. When a sentence is recognised exactly, no such note appears — the note belongs to substitution, not to every world.

The second sentence has an authored change for only 5 of the 11 stored worlds; any other target is refused with a stated reason rather than a borrowed error about a model outage that never happened. Time rewind and branching futures are built and tested, but they are not on the judge's path; they live on a development route only, and nothing in this submission should be read as a claim that a judge can rewind time.

Measured on 13 September 2026 over five sentences against the cloud model: 2.4 model calls per sentence, a median of 143 seconds to a finished world, and a 95th percentile of 259 seconds. Over a separate 30-sentence run the model authored the hero geometry for 0.3 of the attempts; the rest fell back to composed kit pieces or to clay. Creation timings include process and transport overhead, and the judge-path timings were measured over the authored store, not a live model. Geometry is schematic.

A world that passes every structural check does not certify semantic correctness: the runtime proves that the rules are consistent and that the world can be won, never that it means what the sentence meant. The most recent internal review recognised 2 of 10 rendered rooms from the sentence that produced them, which is the measured size of that gap today.

The live model path currently runs only from the author's development machine through the Codex CLI; when that path is absent the hosted deployment answers from the authored store. Independent semantic review and usability evidence from people outside the project remain separate requirements, and neither has been collected.

Third-party libraries, assets, models, datasets and APIs are disclosed with source, author, licence and required notices in `DISCLOSURE.md`.
